Chapter 1
Introduction to the RFP Platform
What Is an RFP and Why Does It Matter?

A Request for Proposal (RFP) is a formal document that organizations use to solicit bids from potential vendors or service providers for a specific project, product, or service. It serves as the backbone of professional procurement processes across virtually every industry. When an organization needs to procure software development services, construction work, professional consulting, IT infrastructure, marketing campaigns, or any other significant business need, the RFP is the standard mechanism for ensuring fairness, transparency, and quality in vendor selection.

The traditional RFP process, however, is notoriously painful. Organizations typically manage RFPs through spreadsheets, email chains, shared documents, and manual tracking. This leads to missed deadlines, lost vendor communications, inconsistent evaluation criteria, difficulty comparing proposals side-by-side, and a general lack of visibility into the procurement pipeline. For vendors, the experience is equally frustrating: tracking multiple RFP opportunities across different organizations, managing submission deadlines, and never knowing where they stand in the evaluation process.

The RFP Platform was built to solve these exact problems. It is a comprehensive, end-to-end solution that digitizes and automates every stage of the procurement lifecycle, from the initial RFP creation through vendor evaluation, contract awarding, and ongoing contract management. By bringing the entire process into a single, unified platform, organizations can dramatically reduce procurement cycle times, improve decision quality, and maintain a complete audit trail of every action.

Platform Overview and Core Philosophy

This platform is a multi-tenant, enterprise-grade SaaS application designed to serve organizations of all sizes. It supports the complete RFP lifecycle, including creation, publishing, vendor invitation, proposal collection, multi-criteria evaluation, approval workflows, and contract management. At its core, the platform follows a philosophy of transparency, efficiency, and fairness, ensuring that every stakeholder, from the procurement team to the vendors, has a clear and intuitive experience.

The platform is self-hosted and open source, meaning organizations can deploy it on their own infrastructure, maintain complete control over their data, and customize it to their specific needs. This is a critical advantage for organizations in regulated industries such as healthcare, government, and finance, where data sovereignty and compliance requirements make cloud-only SaaS solutions unsuitable.

Design Principle
Every feature in this platform was designed with three questions in mind: Does it reduce the time from RFP creation to contract award? Does it improve the quality of vendor selection decisions? Does it provide complete transparency and auditability for compliance requirements?

Chapter 2
Getting Started
Account Setup and Onboarding

Getting started with the RFP Platform is straightforward and designed to get your team productive within minutes. The platform supports individual user registration as well as organization-wide onboarding. New users can sign up through the registration page by providing their name, email address, and a secure password. The platform supports multi-factor authentication (MFA) for enhanced security, and single sign-on (SSO) integration for enterprises that use centralized identity providers.

Upon first login, users are guided through a brief onboarding flow where they can set up their organization profile, configure notification preferences, and customize their experience. Organization administrators can invite additional team members, assign roles, and configure the organization's branding and marketplace settings from the administration panel.

The platform uses a multi-tenant architecture, which means each organization's data is completely isolated from other organizations on the same platform. This is critical for maintaining data confidentiality and compliance, especially for organizations that handle sensitive procurement information. Every query, every file upload, and every interaction is scoped to the user's organization, ensuring that users never see data from other tenants.

Navigating the Dashboard

The dashboard is the central hub of the platform, providing a personalized overview of all procurement activity. When users log in, they are greeted with a welcome message and immediately see the information that matters most to their role. The dashboard is designed to give procurement teams instant visibility into their workload, upcoming deadlines, and critical decisions that need attention.

5 Key Metric Cards

4 Interactive Charts

The dashboard includes a list of recent RFPs with color-coded status badges, deadlines, response counts, and budget information. Users can quickly see which RFPs are in draft, published, under evaluation, or have been awarded. For RFPs approaching their submission deadlines, the dashboard displays amber-colored alert banners to ensure that critical timelines are not missed.

Quick action buttons provide one-click access to the most common tasks: viewing all RFPs, managing vendors, accessing the evaluation dashboard, and reviewing pending approvals. This design ensures that users spend less time navigating and more time making procurement decisions.

This is essential for maintaining process integrity, ensuring compliance, and preventing unauthorized actions. Creating and Publishing RFPs

Creating an RFP on this platform is a structured, guided experience designed to ensure that every critical element of the procurement document is addressed. The wizard walks users through eight distinct steps, each focusing on a specific aspect of the RFP. This eliminates the common problem of incomplete or poorly structured RFPs that lead to ambiguous vendor proposals and difficult evaluation processes.

In the first step, users define the fundamental details of the RFP: a descriptive title, the procurement category (IT Services, Construction, Marketing, etc.), the estimated budget, the confidentiality level (internal, confidential, or restricted), and a comprehensive description of the project requirements. The confidentiality levels are particularly important for organizations that handle sensitive information, as they control who can view the RFP details.

The timeline step allows users to define all critical dates for the procurement process: when the question-and-answer period opens and closes, when vendor proposals are due, when the evaluation period begins and ends, and the target date for making an award decision. These dates are not just informational, they drive the platform's automated reminders and deadline tracking features.

Team assignment is where the RFP owner designates who will be involved in the procurement process. Each team member is assigned a specific role: owner, editor, evaluator, approver, or viewer. This ensures that the right people have the right access at the right time. Evaluators, for example, will only see the RFP in their evaluation dashboard when it reaches the evaluation phase.

The sections and questions step is perhaps the most powerful part of the wizard. Users create structured sections (such as "Company Overview," "Technical Approach," "Pricing," "Past Performance") and within each section, add specific questions that vendors must answer. The platform supports seven question types: text, number, multiple choice, checkbox, file upload, date, and long-form textarea. Sections can be reordered using drag-and-drop, and the entire structure is saved automatically.

The scoring rubric step is where the evaluation criteria are defined. Users create weighted criteria (such as "Technical Approach - 40%", "Price - 30%", "Experience - 20%", "Innovation - 10%"), each with a defined scale range and guidance text that evaluators can reference during scoring. This ensures that all evaluators are using the same framework and that vendor proposals are assessed consistently and fairly.

Timeline, Team, and Vendor Management

The vendor invitation step in the wizard provides two methods for soliciting proposals. Organizations can invite specific vendors by entering their email addresses, which triggers automated invitation emails with secure acceptance tokens. Alternatively, organizations can select vendors from their existing approved vendor list. Each invitation tracks its status (pending, accepted, declined, or expired) and includes an expiry mechanism to ensure the process keeps moving forward.

The RFP detail page provides a comprehensive tabbed interface for managing every aspect of a live RFP. The Overview tab displays all metadata, timeline, assigned team members, invited vendors, and quick statistics. The Sections tab shows all defined sections with question counts. The Q&A tab manages the question-and-answer process where vendors can ask clarifying questions and receive published answers visible to all vendors. The Addenda tab manages document amendments, and the Submissions tab shows all received proposals with their current status.

For organizations that want to cast a wider net, the platform supports publishing RFPs to the public marketplace. This makes the RFP visible to all registered vendors on the platform, not just those specifically invited. Marketplace-published RFPs can receive bids from any qualified vendor, dramatically expanding the pool of potential respondents and increasing competitive pricing pressure.

Chapter 4
The Vendor Marketplace
Discovering Opportunities

The Vendor Marketplace is one of the platform's most powerful features, creating a dynamic ecosystem where organizations and vendors connect. For organizations issuing RFPs, the marketplace provides access to a broader pool of qualified vendors than traditional invitation-only processes. For vendors, it provides a centralized location to discover new business opportunities that match their capabilities, without needing to monitor dozens of different organization websites or procurement portals.

The marketplace landing page features a hero section with real-time statistics: the number of active RFPs, registered vendors, vendor responses received, and pending approvals. Below the hero, two prominent panels display featured RFPs (showing budget, deadline, category, and bid counts) and top-rated vendors (showing verification badges, star ratings, project counts, and specialty areas). This design enables both buyers and sellers to quickly identify the most relevant opportunities and partners.

Advanced search capabilities allow vendors to filter RFPs by category, budget range, deadline, and keywords. Organizations can browse the vendor directory to identify potential bidders before publishing their RFPs, reviewing vendor profiles that include company information, certifications, diversity attributes, performance history, and client reviews.

Vendor Registration and Profiles

Vendors register through a dedicated vendor self-service portal, creating comprehensive profiles that showcase their capabilities to potential buyers. The registration process captures essential business information including company name, contact details, industry categories, relevant certifications, and diversity classifications (minority-owned, women-owned, veteran-owned, disability-owned businesses). These diversity attributes are increasingly important for organizations that have supplier diversity mandates or are required to meet government contracting diversity goals.

Once registered, vendors can enhance their profiles with detailed business descriptions, specialty areas, project portfolios, and performance metrics. The platform tracks vendor performance over time, including on-time delivery rates, quality scores, budget adherence, and total projects completed. This performance data is visible to prospective buyers, helping them make informed decisions about which vendors to invite to their RFPs.

The vendor dashboard provides a dedicated, vendor-centric view of all activity. It includes six tabs: Overview (profile summary and key metrics), Invitations (incoming RFP invitations with accept/decline actions), My Bids (tracking all active, submitted, won, and lost bids), Marketplace (browsing for new opportunities), Team (managing vendor organization users and roles), and Analytics (vendor-specific performance insights). The dashboard also includes a notification bell with unread count, and a comprehensive notification settings modal where vendors can configure email and push notification preferences, quiet hours, and delivery frequency.

The Bidding Process

When a vendor decides to pursue an RFP opportunity, the bidding process is straightforward and well-guided. From the marketplace RFP detail page, vendors can review the complete RFP requirements, timeline, and evaluation criteria before deciding to bid. If they choose to proceed, they enter their bid amount and submit a bid proposal through the platform's structured submission system.

The bid management system tracks every bid's status through its lifecycle: from initial submission through review, shortlisting, and final decision. Vendors receive real-time notifications when their bid status changes, and a built-in messaging system allows them to communicate directly with the procurement team for clarifications and negotiations. This eliminates the communication gaps that frequently occur in traditional RFP processes where vendor questions get lost in email threads.

For organizations, the bid management view shows all received bids with key details at a glance: bid amount, vendor name, submission date, and current status. Procurement teams can sort and filter bids, compare pricing, and begin the evaluation process directly from the bid management interface. The real-time nature of the platform means that as soon as a vendor submits a bid, it appears in the procurement team's view without any delay or manual refresh.

Chapter 5
Proposal Submissions
The Submission Portal

The proposal submission portal is where vendors bring their best work to life. It is a carefully designed, structured interface that guides vendors through responding to every section and question in the RFP. The portal ensures that vendors provide complete, well-organized responses that make the evaluation process smoother and more consistent for the procurement team.

Upon entering the submission portal, vendors see the RFP header with a progress bar that shows how much of the proposal they have completed. A section navigation sidebar on the left displays all sections with completion indicators, showing at a glance which sections are complete, partially complete, or not yet started. This gives vendors a clear sense of progress and helps them prioritize their time effectively.

The portal renders each question according to its type with appropriate input controls. Text and number inputs include real-time validation with inline error messages. Textarea fields provide ample space for detailed responses. Multiple choice and checkbox questions use clean, selectable option lists. File upload questions allow vendors to attach supporting documents, case studies, or portfolio samples. Date picker questions ensure consistent date formatting, and the platform even supports data integration buttons that allow vendors to connect to external data sources to pre-fill answers automatically.

One of the most practical features of the submission portal is the draft auto-save capability. Vendors can work on their proposals over multiple sessions without losing any progress. The platform automatically saves drafts at regular intervals, and vendors can also manually save their work at any point using the "Save Draft" button. When they return to the portal, they can resume exactly where they left off. This is critical for complex RFPs that may take days or weeks to complete, as it eliminates the risk of losing work due to browser crashes, network issues, or simply needing to gather additional information before continuing.

Validation System
The portal includes a comprehensive validation system that checks responses against defined rules in real-time: business name format validation, numeric range checks, currency formatting, and regex pattern matching. A Validation Alerts panel shows both section-level and field-level errors, ensuring vendors submit complete and correctly formatted proposals before the deadline.
E-Signatures and Validation

Many procurement processes require vendors to formally sign their proposals, either to confirm the accuracy of the information provided or to accept specific terms and conditions. The platform includes a built-in electronic signature system that eliminates the need for external signing tools or physical signatures.

When an RFP requires a signature, vendors are presented with an e-signature modal that captures their name, email address, job title, and a handwritten signature drawn directly on a signature canvas. The platform generates a SHA-256 cryptographic hash of the signed document content, creating a tamper-evident record that links the signature to the exact document content at the time of signing. Vendors must also explicitly accept the terms before the signature is recorded. This process provides a level of security and verifiability that meets the requirements of most procurement and contract processes.

The validation system operates on multiple levels. At the field level, individual questions validate inputs as the vendor types, providing immediate feedback on formatting requirements or value constraints. At the section level, the system checks that all required questions within a section have been answered before the vendor can mark the section as complete. At the proposal level, the system performs a comprehensive check before allowing submission, ensuring that every required section and question has been addressed.

Chapter 6
Evaluation and Scoring
Weighted Scoring Rubrics

The evaluation system is where the RFP Platform truly differentiates itself from simple document collection tools. Rather than relying on subjective impressions or inconsistent evaluation methods, the platform provides a structured, quantitative approach to proposal assessment that leads to more objective and defensible vendor selection decisions.

Each RFP has its own scoring rubric, defined during the creation process, that specifies the evaluation criteria and their relative weights. For example, a software development RFP might weight Technical Architecture at 30%, Development Methodology at 25%, Pricing at 20%, Team Qualifications at 15%, and Innovation at 10%. These weights directly influence the final composite score, ensuring that the criteria the organization considers most important have the greatest impact on the outcome.

Evaluators access the Evaluation Dashboard, which shows all RFPs assigned to them for review. The dashboard has three filter tabs: "Needs My Evaluation" (RFPs where the user hasn't scored yet, with unread indicator dots), "All Evaluations" (complete list), and "Completed" (only finished evaluations). Each entry shows the RFP title, status, submission count, evaluator progress with visual progress bars, average scores with color coding, and evaluation deadlines.

Slider-Based Scoring
Evaluators use intuitive slider inputs to score each submission against every rubric criterion, with the scale range and guidance text visible for reference.
Per-Criterion Notes
Each score can include detailed notes explaining the evaluator's reasoning, providing transparency and supporting consensus discussions.
Save and Submit
Evaluators can save scores in progress and return later, or submit their final evaluation when complete. Submitted scores can still be edited until finalization.
Progress Tracking
Real-time progress indicators show how many evaluators have completed their reviews, with visual progress bars on every RFP entry.
Blind Evaluation and Consensus

For organizations that require the highest level of evaluation objectivity, the platform supports blind evaluation mode. When enabled, vendor names and identifying information are hidden from evaluators during the scoring process. Evaluators see only the proposal content, allowing them to assess each submission purely on its merits without any potential bias from brand recognition, prior relationships, or other non-content factors. This is particularly valuable for public sector organizations and highly regulated industries where procurement fairness is subject to external audit.

The Consensus tab provides a powerful analysis of evaluator agreement. For each submission and each criterion, the platform calculates the mean score, standard deviation, and agreement level across all evaluators. High standard deviations flag criteria where evaluators disagree significantly, indicating areas that may need further discussion. The system also identifies statistical outliers, individual scores that deviate substantially from the group average, which may represent either a genuinely different perspective or a potential error that should be reviewed.

This consensus analysis serves multiple purposes. For procurement leaders, it identifies where the evaluation team is aligned and where there is disagreement that needs to be resolved before making a final decision. For auditors, it provides evidence that the evaluation was conducted rigorously and that any final decision was based on considered judgment rather than a single evaluator's opinion. For the evaluators themselves, it creates accountability, knowing that their scores will be visible in the context of the full team's assessments.

Side-by-Side Comparison

The Comparison tab provides a comprehensive side-by-side view of all submitted proposals across every evaluation criterion. Each vendor's scores are displayed in columns, with weighted totals calculated automatically. This visualization makes it immediately clear which vendors excel in specific areas and which have the strongest overall proposals according to the defined evaluation framework.

The comparison view is particularly valuable during the decision-making phase, when procurement leaders need to justify their vendor selection to stakeholders or approval bodies. Rather than relying on summaries or individual evaluator notes, decision-makers can see the complete quantitative picture, understand trade-offs between vendors (for example, Vendor A scores higher on technical approach but Vendor B offers better pricing), and make data-driven award decisions. The CSV export capability allows this comparison data to be extracted for inclusion in procurement reports or presentations to senior leadership.

Approval Workflows and Contracts
Multi-Stage Approvals

Many organizations require that significant procurement decisions go through multiple levels of review and approval before a contract can be awarded. The platform's approval workflow system supports this requirement with configurable multi-stage approval chains that mirror an organization's actual governance structure. Rather than forcing a one-size-fits-all approval process, the platform allows each organization to define the stages that reflect their specific policies and regulations.

The standard approval stages flow from Draft through Legal Review, Budget Review, Publish approval, Evaluation Complete confirmation, Award approval, and finally Contract approval. However, organizations can customize these stages to match their internal processes. Each stage can have one or more assigned approvers, and each approval request tracks its priority level (low, medium, high, urgent, or critical) with color-coded indicators.

→

→

→

Final approval to release to vendors
→

→

The Approvals page provides a centralized view of all pending, approved, and rejected requests. Approvers can filter by status, priority, stage, and search by RFP title. The approval table shows the RFP title, current stage, status, priority with color indicators, the person who requested the approval, the assigned approver, the budget amount, and the request timestamp. Quick action dialogs allow approvers to approve, reject, view details, or add comments without leaving the page.

SLA tracking is built into the approval system. Each approval request has a default service level agreement (typically 48 hours), and the platform's background scheduler monitors for overdue approvals. When an approval exceeds its SLA, the system automatically generates security alerts and notifications to ensure that bottlenecks are identified and addressed promptly. This automated monitoring prevents the common problem of procurement processes stalling because an approver is unavailable or has overlooked a request.

Contract Lifecycle Management

Once a vendor has been selected through the evaluation and approval process, the platform transitions seamlessly into contract management. The award workflow is a three-step process: first, the RFP owner selects the winning submission; then, they enter contract details including start and end dates, contract value, and any special notes; finally, they confirm the award, which creates a formal contract record in the system.

The Contracts page displays all contracts in a comprehensive table with columns for RFP title, vendor name, contract status (draft, active, expired, terminated, or completed), start and end dates, contract value, the person who awarded the contract, and the creation date. Status badges are color-coded for instant visual recognition. Users can search contracts by keyword, filter by status, and filter by date range to find specific contracts quickly.

Contract status is tracked throughout the contract lifecycle. A contract starts in "draft" status when it is first created through the award workflow, moves to "active" when it is formally executed, and can progress through "expired" (when the end date passes without renewal), "terminated" (if either party ends the contract early), or "completed" (when all deliverables have been met and the contract is formally closed). This lifecycle tracking provides organizations with a complete picture of their contractual commitments at any point in time.

Chapter 8
Communication and Collaboration
Real-Time Messaging

Effective communication is the foundation of successful procurement, and the platform provides a comprehensive real-time messaging system that keeps all stakeholders connected. The messaging interface features a thread list panel on the left showing all conversations, and a message view on the right displaying the full conversation history with message bubbles, timestamps, and read receipts.

Each message thread can be associated with a specific RFP, vendor, or user, providing context for every conversation. The thread list shows the conversation partner's avatar, name, the last message preview, timestamp, and unread count badges. Online status indicators let users know when their contacts are available for immediate communication. The messaging system supports file attachments, allowing users to share documents, specifications, or clarifications directly within the conversation context.

Read receipts provide visibility into message delivery and engagement. A single check mark indicates the message has been delivered, while double check marks confirm the recipient has read the message. This eliminates the uncertainty that plagues email-based communication where senders never know if their message was received or read. Users can also mark conversations as read or unread, archive them for future reference, or delete them entirely.

Q&A, Announcements, and Calendar

The Q&A management system is a critical collaboration tool during the RFP response period. Vendors can submit questions about the RFP requirements, and the procurement team can respond with answers that can be published to all vendors (ensuring equal access to information) or kept private (for vendor-specific clarifications). The Q&A interface shows the RFP title, question text, answer, status (pending, answered, or published), public/private badge, vendor name, and timestamps. This structured approach to Q&A eliminates the chaos of vendor questions arriving via multiple email threads and ensures that all vendors receive the same information.

The announcements system allows organizations to broadcast important information to all users or specific groups. Announcements support priority levels (info, warning, error), categories, file attachments, expiry dates, and pinning for persistent visibility. The announcement creation dialog includes fields for title, content, priority, category, attachments, expiry date, and a pin toggle. Users can filter announcements by priority and search by keyword, and the notification bell shows unread announcement counts.

Integrated Calendar
The platform includes a full calendar interface with monthly navigation and event support for deadlines, meetings, milestones, presentations, and review sessions. Events are color-coded by type and can include location details and video meeting URLs. Users can switch between calendar and list views, and RSVP status (accepted, tentative, declined) is tracked for meeting events.

The notification system is comprehensive and user-configurable. It supports 14 distinct notification types including new RFP alerts, bid acceptance notifications, Q&A answers, approval requests and decisions, RFP published and closed notices, deadline reminders (shown in red for urgency), contract updates, vendor registrations, evaluation completions, announcements, and system messages. Each notification type has a distinctive badge variant for quick visual identification. Users can mark individual notifications as read, mark all as read, delete individual notifications, or clear all at once. Filter tabs allow viewing all notifications or only unread ones.

Chapter 9
Analytics and Insights
Dashboard Analytics

Data-driven decision making is at the heart of modern procurement, and the platform provides a rich analytics system that transforms raw procurement data into actionable insights. The Analytics page offers a date range selector (All Time, 7 Days, 30 Days, 90 Days) that applies across all metrics and charts, allowing users to focus on the time period most relevant to their analysis.

The analytics dashboard is organized into four metric groups. First, the RFP Metrics group shows total RFPs, published count, those in evaluation, awarded count, and average cycle time (measuring the typical duration from RFP creation to contract award). Second, the Vendor Metrics group displays total vendors, active vendors, average response rate, and top performers ranked by win rate and average evaluation score. Third, the Financial Metrics group presents total budget across all RFPs, total awarded value, remaining budget, and average award value. Fourth, the Timeline Metrics group tracks average creation-to-publish time, average publish-to-award time, and average evaluation duration.

RFP Volume by Category

These visualizations serve multiple audiences within the organization. Procurement managers use them to identify bottlenecks in the process and optimize cycle times. Finance teams use the budget and award data to forecast spending and track procurement ROI. Executive leadership uses the trend data to understand procurement scale and efficiency over time. And vendor relationship managers use the response rate and vendor performance data to strengthen their supply chain.

Export Capabilities

The platform provides CSV export functionality across all major data areas, enabling users to extract data for external analysis, reporting, or integration with other business systems. Exports are available for RFP data (including all metadata, status, and timeline information), vendor data (including contact information, categories, certifications, and performance scores), evaluation data (including all evaluator scores, consensus calculations, and comments), and analytics data (including all metrics and calculated values).

Each export file is automatically named with a date stamp (for example, "rfps-export-2025-01-15.csv") for easy organization and version tracking. The CSV format ensures compatibility with virtually any data analysis tool, spreadsheet application, or business intelligence platform. Security, Compliance, and Automation
Enterprise-Grade Security

Security is not an afterthought in this platform; it is woven into every layer of the architecture. The platform implements multiple security mechanisms that work together to protect sensitive procurement data, ensure the integrity of the evaluation process, and maintain compliance with industry regulations. For organizations in healthcare, government, finance, and other regulated sectors, these security features are not just nice-to-have but are essential requirements for adopting any procurement technology.

Data Protection
Multi-tenant data isolation, AES-256 encryption utilities, SHA-256 file hashing for integrity, legal hold support, and configurable retention policies.
Audit and Compliance
Complete audit logging of all user actions, HMAC-SHA256 webhook signing, security alert tracking, and compliance framework support (SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, HIPAA).

The multi-tenant architecture ensures that each organization's data is completely isolated at the database query level. Every API request is scoped to the authenticated user's tenant, making it impossible for one organization to access another organization's data, even if they share the same physical infrastructure. This architectural isolation is stronger than application-level access controls because it operates at the data layer, providing defense in depth.

The platform's audit logging system records every significant action: who performed the action, when it was performed, what changed, and from where. This audit trail is essential for compliance reporting, incident investigation, and demonstrating process integrity to external auditors. The administration panel provides a dedicated Audit Log viewer with powerful filtering capabilities, allowing administrators to search logs by user, action type, date range, and resource.

Webhooks and Automated Workflows

Automation is what transforms a good procurement platform into a great one. The platform includes a sophisticated automation layer that handles routine tasks, enforces deadlines, and keeps all stakeholders informed without manual intervention. This automation reduces the administrative burden on procurement teams and ensures that no critical deadline or action is missed.

The webhook system allows organizations to integrate the RFP Platform with their existing business systems, such as ERP, CRM, accounting, or project management tools. The platform supports 11 distinct event types that can trigger webhook calls: RFP created, published, closed, awarded, and archived; submission created and awarded; vendor registered and approved; evaluation completed; and contract created and status changed. Each webhook endpoint is configured with an HMAC-SHA256 signing secret, allowing the receiving system to verify that the webhook payload is authentic and has not been tampered with.

Background Scheduler
A built-in scheduler runs every 60 seconds to handle three critical automated tasks: (1) Auto-closing expired RFPs when their submission deadline passes, (2) Sending deadline reminders at 7 days, 3 days, and 1 day before submission deadlines, and (3) Detecting overdue approval requests and generating SLA breach alerts.

The deadline reminder system is particularly valuable for keeping procurement processes on track. As RFP submission deadlines approach, the system automatically sends reminders to both the procurement team and the invited vendors. The three-tier reminder schedule (7 days, 3 days, and 1 day before deadline) provides ample warning without being overwhelming. These automated reminders eliminate the need for procurement managers to manually track and send deadline notifications, reducing the risk of missed deadlines and ensuring that vendors have every opportunity to submit their proposals on time.

The auto-close feature ensures data integrity by automatically transitioning published RFPs to "closed" status when their submission deadline passes. This prevents vendors from submitting late proposals and signals to the procurement team that the collection phase is complete and evaluation can begin. Combined with the SLA monitoring for approvals, these automation features create a self-driving procurement process that keeps moving forward even when team members are focused on other priorities.
